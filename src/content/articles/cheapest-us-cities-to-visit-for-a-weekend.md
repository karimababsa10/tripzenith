<h1>Cheapest US Cities to Visit for a Weekend</h1><br><p>When it comes to planning a weekend getaway, I’ve always found that the thrill of exploring a new city doesn’t have to come with a hefty price tag. As a frequent traveler, I love discovering the cheapest U.S. cities to visit for a weekend — places that offer charm, unforgettable experiences, and, most importantly, a budget-friendly way to unwind. Here’s a rundown of some of my favorite spots, along with what you can expect in terms of costs, things to do, and, of course, food!</p><h2>1. New Orleans, Louisiana</h2><p><strong>Approximate Weekend Cost:</strong> $400</p><p><strong>Accommodation:</strong> You can find budget hotels or charming hostels starting at $70 a night.</p><p><strong>Food:</strong> Be sure to try po'boys and gumbo for around $15 per meal. Enjoying a beignet at Café du Monde is a must, costing only about $3.</p><p><strong>Transportation:</strong> The streetcar is just $1.25 per ride, making it easy to traverse the city.</p><p><strong>Things to Do:</strong> Walk around the French Quarter and stop by Jackson Square. There’s always live jazz in the streets, and enjoy the art from local vendors. Plus, the history tours are usually under $20.</p><p><strong>Why I Would Choose It:</strong> The energy in New Orleans is incomparable. The mix of culture, music, and cuisine makes it a weekend worth any traveler’s while.</p><h2>2. San Diego, California</h2><p><strong>Approximate Weekend Cost:</strong> $500</p><p><strong>Accommodation:</strong> Affordable beach hostels start at $60 a night. For a budget hotel, expect around $120.</p><p><strong>Food:</strong> Tacos are a must and can be found for about $4 each. Dining by the beach adds to the experience but expect to pay around $20 for a decent meal.</p><p><strong>Transportation:</strong> A local trolley day pass is around $6, which can take you around the city’s top attractions.</p><p><strong>Things to Do:</strong> Enjoy Balboa Park, where many museums have free admission on certain days. The beach is, of course, free — just bring a towel!</p><p><strong>Why I Wouldn’t Choose It:</strong> It can be more on the pricier side if you’re looking to dine fancy. But if you stick to casual eats and beach days, you can truly enjoy San Diego on a budget.</p><h2>3. Austin, Texas</h2><p><strong>Approximate Weekend Cost:</strong> $350</p><p><strong>Accommodation:</strong> Affordable accommodations can be found for about $90 a night.</p><p><strong>Food:</strong> Famous for BBQ, you can easily grab a brisket plate for $10. Food trucks are also a great budget option — meals for about $8.</p><p><strong>Transportation:</strong> Walking is the best way to get around downtown, but the bus is affordable at $1.25.</p><p><strong>Things to Do:</strong> Enjoy live music in the parks and visit the South Congress Avenue for local shops. Don’t forget to check out the bats under the Congress Avenue Bridge at dusk!</p><p><strong>Why I Would Choose It:</strong> The live music scene and outdoor vibes make Austin a unique experience for every traveler.</p><h2>4. Portland, Oregon</h2><p><strong>Approximate Weekend Cost:</strong> $500</p><p><strong>Accommodation:</strong> Hostels start at about $50 a night, while hotels can range around $120.</p><p><strong>Food:</strong> Savor food at the famous food trucks, where you can find delicious options for $10. A café latte might cost you around $4.</p><p><strong>Transportation:</strong> The TriMet day pass is $5, allowing unlimited use of buses and light rail.</p><p><strong>Things to Do:</strong> The Portland Japanese Garden and waterfront parks are both free. Stroll through Powells City of Books, which is an experience all its own!</p><p><strong>Why I Wouldn’t Choose It:</strong> While there’s a lot of charm, the weather can be unpredictable. It’s mostly rain; something to keep in mind!</p><h2>5. Memphis, Tennessee</h2><p><strong>Approximate Weekend Cost:</strong> $300</p><p><strong>Accommodation:</strong> Rates start as low as $80 for budget hotels.</p><p><strong>Food:</strong> Indulge in some of the best barbecue for $10. The breakfast joints are great too, averaging about $8.</p><p><strong>Transportation:</strong> The trolley system is a budget-friendly way to explore the city at $1 per ride.</p><p><strong>Things to Do:</strong> Take a walk along Beale Street or enjoy the National Civil Rights Museum (entrance fee around $16). The free live music around town keeps the spirit alive.</p><p><strong>Why I Would Choose It:</strong> The blend of music and food makes for rich experiences. Plus, the hospitality I encountered is worth the trip.</p><h2>6. Savannah, Georgia</h2><p><strong>Approximate Weekend Cost:</strong> $400</p><p><strong>Accommodation:</strong> Rooms can be found for about $90 a night.</p><p><strong>Food:</strong> Enjoy Southern cuisine at prices around $15. The little cafés along the squares offer coffee for $3.</p><p><strong>Transportation:</strong> It’s a walkable city, but you might want to budget about $5 for streetcars.</p><p><strong>Things to Do:</strong> Explore Forsyth Park or take a free walking tour to soak in the charm and history of Savannah.</p><p><strong>Why I Would Choose It:</strong> The stunning architecture and cobblestone streets create a romantic atmosphere perfect for every traveler.</p><h2>7. Chicago, Illinois</h2><p><strong>Approximate Weekend Cost:</strong> $600</p><p><strong>Accommodation:</strong> Prices average around $120 for budget hotels.</p><p><strong>Food:</strong> Chicago-style hot dogs can be found for less than $5, while deep-dish pizza sits at around $20.</p><p><strong>Transportation:</strong> The CTA pass is $10 for unlimited rides in the city for a day.</p><p><strong>Things to Do:</strong> Visit Millennium Park and take a selfie by the Bean. The Art Institute of Chicago has free days all year!</p><p><strong>Why I Wouldn’t Choose It:</strong> The city can be overwhelming with its size, but the attractions are genuinely top-notch for the price you pay.</p><h2>Comparison Table</h2><table><tr><th>City</th><th>Approx.
